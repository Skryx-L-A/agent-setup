### 4b. Optional: one anonymized cross-read

Skip this for routine calls. Run it when the decision is expensive to reverse, when the three
voices disagree flatly, or when two of them agree for reasons that sound thin.

Send each voice the other two positions back, stripped of their role labels — "Position A",
"Position B" — and ask which one is stronger and why. The labels are what you are hiding, and
hiding them is the point: a Skeptic told it is reading the Pragmatist argues with the role, not
with the argument. Karpathy's `llm-council` anonymizes for the same reason, so that a model
"can't play favorites when judging their outputs".

```text
Two other members of the council answered the same question. Their roles are withheld.

Position A:
[verbatim]

Position B:
[verbatim]

Which is stronger, and on which specific point is it stronger? If one of them changes your own
position, say so and say what changed it. Under 150 words.
```

What you are looking for is not a vote. It is the case where a voice abandons its own position
after reading another — that is the strongest signal this skill produces, and it is worth more
than three confident opinions that never met. Carry it into the synthesis explicitly.
